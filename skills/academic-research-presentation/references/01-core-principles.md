# Core Principles

## Research slides are argument + evidence
Each substantive slide should answer a scientific question or advance one claim.
Evidence—not ornamental layout—should occupy the visual center.

## Scientific-object-driven
Choose figure, table, equation, algorithm, architecture, qualitative evidence,
or diagnostic plot first. Layout follows the object.

## Canvas-first
Normal text sits directly on the canvas. A border is used only when the
boundary itself conveys scientific grouping.

## Evidence hierarchy
Prefer:
1. direct experimental evidence;
2. source method visual/equation/algorithm;
3. faithful reconstruction from exact data;
4. explanatory schematic verified against source;
5. concise text.

Do not replace 1–3 with 5 because prose is easier to generate.

## One argument unit per slide
Multiple observations may coexist only when they support the same central claim.

## Epistemic discipline
Mark mentally and verbally:
- FACT FROM SOURCE
- INTERPRETATION
- SPECULATION / OUR IDEA

Never present interpretation as directly measured fact.

## Technical detail is not clutter by default
Do not remove equations, update rules, reward definitions, data splits,
error bars, train/inference distinctions, or ablation conditions merely to make
the slide minimalist. Redesign them.

## Slide count is subordinate to scientific completeness
Do not clip or shrink important evidence to stay within a guessed slide count.
Add a slide or move secondary content to backup.

## Human researcher test
Reject layouts dominated by:
- equal cards for unequal ideas;
- decorative rounded rectangles;
- generic icons;
- dashboard grids;
- whitespace that displaces evidence.
