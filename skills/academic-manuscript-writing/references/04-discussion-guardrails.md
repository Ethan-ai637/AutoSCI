# Discussion guardrails

## Use an interpretation ladder

For each main finding:

1. **Observation** — exact result supported by study evidence.
2. **Immediate interpretation** — what that result may mean within the studied system.
3. **Context** — relation to supplied prior literature.
4. **Alternative explanation** — plausible competing account when relevant.
5. **Boundary** — population/design/measurement limitation.
6. **Next test** — what evidence would discriminate among explanations.

Do not skip directly from observation to mechanism.

## Mechanistic claims

Use “supports”, “is consistent with”, or “suggests” when evidence is indirect. Reserve mechanistic language such as “mediates”, “drives”, or “causes” for designs that actually identify that relation.

## Generalization

Keep claims bounded to the studied population, environment, dataset, task, organism, instrument, or time period unless broader evidence is supplied.

## Limitations

Prioritize limitations that can change interpretation, not boilerplate. Examples include selection bias, measurement validity, missing controls, multiplicity, model misspecification, domain shift, low event counts, unblinded assessment, or post hoc subgrouping.
