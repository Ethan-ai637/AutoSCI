# Claim-language calibration

## Descriptive

Use for direct observations:

- “was higher/lower”
- “increased/decreased”
- “the estimate was…”
- “we observed…”

## Associational

Use when design does not identify causality:

- “was associated with”
- “correlated with”
- “co-varied with”
- “participants with X had…”

## Causal

Use only when the design and analysis support it:

- “increased” as an intervention effect
- “caused”
- “reduced risk”
- “led to”

Even in randomized studies, avoid causal claims outside the randomized contrast or when major protocol deviations/analysis issues break the interpretation.

## Interpretive strength

From weaker to stronger:

- “may reflect”
- “is consistent with”
- “suggests”
- “supports”
- “demonstrates”

Choose based on evidence, not desired tone.

## Words that often need evidence beyond significance

Be cautious with:

- robust
- substantial
- meaningful
- clinically relevant
- state-of-the-art
- superior
- generalizable
- safe
- effective
- scalable
- mechanistic

These may be appropriate, but each needs a defined evidentiary basis.
